# Noble — delivery 78, second wave

Bounded road-production history: M10, M12, M400, M600. Claude external card was absent and no existing Noble line identities were found at start. All four are acknowledged by the manufacturer's current indexed site. M12 GTO/GTO-3/GTO-3R are grouped in one family pending complete technical-version evidence; M400 is separately named but its relationship to M12 is explicit. Rossion Q1 is not an extension of Noble production.

Production-date conflicts remain unresolved: M12 starts 2000 versus 2001 and ends 2007 versus 2008; both bounds null. M400 first-production references differ 2004/2006, so start null and agreeing end 2007 retained. M600 prototype presentation in 2009, production-ready press report 2011 and references to 2010 must not be collapsed into a guessed factory date; start null. An old review does not prove the end of production. M10 1999–2000 is the sourced historical bound; its four-cylinder prototype is not its production motor.

M500 is **held out**, not declared impossible or permanently cancelled: the OEM indexed page calls the pictured car the first pre-production prototype and promises a late-2024 release. That promise, and a photograph of the prototype, do not establish actual customer production by 2026. M14/M15 and M12 GTC are likewise held out pending positive production evidence; secondary infobox dates and development test drives are insufficient here. This is a bounded-source result, not proof no additional cars were produced.

Three M600 rows are the three OEM-named body/finish forms Coupé, CarbonSport and Speedster. The manufacturer explicitly states shared running gear/performance; these are not three separately invented engine versions. The common factory page directly publishes 4439 cm³, eight cylinders, Maximum Net Power 492 kW, length 4360 mm, wheelbase 2540 mm, weight 1198 kg, combined fuel consumption 14 l/100 km. The exact model year and publication revision are unknown. The site's quoted weight has **unknown mass basis**, explicitly labelled and reviewed; it is not silently called kerb/dry. Combined consumption has an **unspecified measurement protocol**, not an assumed NEDC/WLTP cycle. PowerStandard remains null because a net-power label does not establish DIN/ECE. Imperial torque and estimated mph speed are not converted; the quoted 0–120 mph run is not an acceleration-to-100 figure. Other source claims about prototype weight/performance and conversions are not merged.
